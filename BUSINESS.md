# nance.it — Project & Business Model

nance.it is **100% open source** (Apache-2.0). This document explains the model behind the project: how it is licensed, how it sustains itself, and what is and isn't reserved. It is a high-level model, not a go-to-market plan, and names no specific clients.

## Fully open, no open-core

There is no proprietary tier, no feature gate, no open-core split — the entire codebase is Apache-2.0. The commercial engine is **not the software**; it is the **expertise required to model a quality & governance system inside it**.

The core insight: an organization's real pain is rarely "we lack a tool." It is *"we cannot translate our obligations — ISO 9001, sector standards, contractual requirements, internal governance — into an operating model."* That translation is high-value work, and every engagement doubles as product discovery: the capabilities a client needs to model their system are the capabilities the open project needs to mature.

## The flywheel

1. A client buys **implementation + modeling assistance**.
2. Modeling surfaces missing capabilities → built as **paid engineering hours**.
3. New capabilities land **upstream in the open core** → the product improves for everyone.
4. A stronger product attracts the next client → back to step 1.

The client funds the roadmap; the roadmap grows the market; the market brings clients. What an open-core model would lock behind a paywall, this model converts into billable engineering that compounds the public asset.

## Licensing & IP

- **License:** Apache-2.0 for the entire codebase — frictionless enterprise legal review, an explicit patent grant, and no copyleft concerns for client-side integrations.
- **Trademark as the moat:** the **nance** name and the **nance.it** domain are reserved. Anyone may fork and run the code; nobody else may sell "nance." In a fully open model, brand is the exclusionary asset.
- **Upstream-first customization:** client-requested features are developed in the open by default. Client-specific *configuration and models* (their processes, requirements, procedures) remain their confidential data — the separation between engine (public) and modeled content (private) is what makes "fully open" safe for enterprise adopters.

## Offer structure

- **Implementation** (project-based) — deploy nance.it and stand up the client's first modeled domain: installation, data-model seeding, identity/document integration, and training of internal quality roles.
- **Support plans** (recurring) — monthly pools bundling **engineering hours** (features, integrations, upgrades, shipped upstream) and **modeling hours** (quality/process professionals helping model the QMS/governance system: requirement registers, procedures, competence matrices, event/payload design, audit preparation). The pairing is the differentiator: most OSS support sells insurance; nance.it sells the solution to the actual pain, with engineering capacity attached.
- **Standalone consulting** — audit preparation, governance-gap assessments, process redesign; billable independently and the natural entry door.

## Why fully open source

It removes the procurement wall (an organization can pilot without a purchase order, then buy services on demonstrated value), aligns incentives visibly (paid hours become public improvements, which de-risks lock-in objections), and lets a small expert team out-*expertise* incumbents in a niche, with the tool as proof of method.

The trade-off is deliberate: the strategy targets a **sustainable services business**, not an asset sale. The durable assets are brand, recurring support contracts, community, and the team.
