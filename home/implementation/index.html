    Running in 7 weeks — the proven playbook¶

    Fixed scope, key users trained, MVP live. The same playbook now running in the Power Transformer Repairs & Services unit can be mirrored for your department.

    Three steps to get started¶

    Step 1 — Discovery workshop¶

    Week 0. One hour a day for a full working week with your key users: map processes, obligations, and the first scope to model.

    A week of one-hour sessions rather than one long day is deliberate. Between sessions, people check what they claimed against how the work is actually done — and that correction is where most of the value of the workshop comes from.

    This is also the moment to capture your ROI baseline, while the right people are already in the room.

    Step 2 — Mirrored implementation¶

    Weeks 1–7. The proven playbook below, adapted to your department.

    Step 3 — Shared support plan¶

    Ongoing. Engineering and quality-management hours. Your needs join the roadmap that all units accelerate together.

    The seven weeks¶

    Week Focus Duration
    W1 Onboarding workshop with stakeholders — including software integrations and digitalization compliance 1 week
    W2 Organization module setup 1 week
    W3 Portfolio module setup 1 week
    W4–W5 CRM module setup (SLAs) 2 weeks
    W6–W7 Key-user training — Operation · Talent · Workspace 2 weeks

    Why this order¶

    It is the same order the Start here page gives you, and for the same reason: every mandatory field in a form points at something registered earlier. Organization before Portfolio because products and scopes belong to business units. Portfolio before CRM because an SLA purchases payloads built from events and product scopes. Operation, Talent and Workspace last because they consume everything the first three weeks produced.

    Attempting a different order does not fail loudly — it fails as empty selectors and rework.

    What is expected from your side¶

    The playbook holds to seven weeks when three conditions are met, and slips when they are not.

    Key-user availability. The modelling work needs the people who actually know how the work is done, not their delegates. This is the single most common cause of a slipped timeline — domain experts are, by definition, the people whose time is hardest to book.

    A named internal owner, appointed before deployment. The system needs someone accountable for the Broker and Quality Manager functions after the engagement ends. A system deployed without an internal owner gradually becomes unused, and that is a failure mode no implementation quality can prevent.

    Scope discipline. The first pass models one business unit and one process family. The instinct to model everything at once is understandable and reliably costly — it delays the moment when key users can see their own operation in the system, which is what makes them adopt it.

    What you have at week 7¶

    • Your organisation structure, portfolio and contracts registered
    • One process family modelled end to end — events, processes, workflows, tasks, procedures
    • Competences defined and your first people certified against them
    • Key users trained on the modules they will own
    • An MVP live, with execution running through it

    From there, additional process families follow the same pattern, at a pace your team sets.

    The ask¶

    Agree on the discovery workshop date. Everything else follows from what is found there.

    Before that conversation, the whole product is available to evaluate: the public demo with sample data, and a blank instance at /app/mvp/ if you want to try modelling something of your own first.
