# Three seats, three wins

One platform — read by the board as governance, by engineering as method, and by IT as clean
architecture.

## Business Unit Manager

**Governance visibility across departments.** Obligations, owners and controls are registered
objects rather than a set of departmental practices you have to ask about.

**Audit readiness as a by-product of operations.** Evidence accumulates while work happens.
Preparing for an audit becomes retrieval instead of a project.

**Lower risk when outsourcing or scaling teams.** The reason is the platform's own origin
story: work that is written down can be delegated, and work that is not, cannot. Explicit
procedures and certified competences are what make added capacity safe.

*Start with:* [The governance gap](gap.md), then [The return](roi.md).

## Department Manager

**Explicit procedures for every task.** Including the part most systems miss — the same task
under a different requirement set is a different procedure, with a different execution time.

**Only certified people staffed on critical work.** Not as a policy people are asked to
respect, but as a constraint the staffing selector enforces.

**Onboarding time cut, and knowledge stays when people move.** The training path is part of
the competence record, so a departure removes a person rather than a capability.

*Start with:* [How it works](how-it-works.md), then [Start here](../start-here.md) and model
one process family in the demo.

## Digital / IT Lead

**Open code, fully auditable, Apache 2.0.** Explicit patent grant, no copyleft contamination
for your internal integrations, and a legal review that is short because the licence is one
your organisation has already approved many times.

**No licence procurement, standard web stack.** A pilot needs no purchase order. The platform
fits an existing automation landscape rather than requiring its own.

**Integration-ready.** Identity and document infrastructure integration is part of the
implementation scope, and the data model is documented publicly — this site.

**Continuity you can verify.** Source, schema and documentation are all in your hands, which
makes vendor risk a sourcing question rather than an existential one. The reasoning is in
[The business model](business-model.md#7-who-maintains-it-and-what-happens-if-neun-stops).

*Start with:* [The business model](business-model.md), then the public repository.

## The common thread

These are not three different pitches. They are three readings of the same property: when the
operating model is explicit and connected, the manager gets visibility, the engineer gets
method, and IT gets a system it can reason about — without anyone maintaining a separate
artefact for the other two.
