# The governance gap

Three versions of the truth exist in every engineering organisation, and they do not match.

## What documents say

- Procedures live in scattered files and drives
- They are updated after the fact — if at all
- They are disconnected from daily execution

The document is written once, usually to answer an audit finding, and then stops tracking
reality. Everyone knows this; nobody has time to fix it, because fixing it means rewriting
documents that nothing depends on.

## What people actually do

- Critical know-how lives in a few heads
- It leaves when they leave
- Every onboarding starts from zero

This is the version that produces the work. It is also the one nobody can read, review or
transfer. The organisation's real operating model exists, but only as an oral tradition.

## What auditors find

- Evidence reconstructed retrospectively
- Sampling instead of full traceability
- Findings arriving months after the fact

An audit that samples cannot tell you whether the other ninety-five per cent was compliant. A
finding that arrives in the next quarter cannot prevent anything — it can only document that
prevention failed.

---

**The distance between these three is where quality escapes, audits hurt — and knowledge is
silently lost.**

## Why the usual fixes do not close it

**Writing more documents** widens the gap rather than closing it: more text disconnected from
execution means more to maintain and more to contradict.

**Auditing more often** samples the same undocumented reality more frequently. It raises cost
without raising certainty.

**Buying a document management system** organises the first column. The other two are
untouched — the procedure is easier to find, and still nothing stops an uncertified person
from executing the task, and still nothing records that they did.

## What actually closes it

The three columns converge only when the document *is* the execution path — when the
procedure a person follows is the same object that certified them to follow it, and the same
object that records that they did.

That is a modelling problem before it is a software problem, and it is why the platform is
inseparable from the method: you close the gap by connecting requirement, procedure,
competence and execution into one chain, so that all three versions of the truth are
generated from the same source.
