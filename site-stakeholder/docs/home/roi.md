# ROI you can verify — not a promise

**There are no invented percentages on this page.**

Vendors in this category routinely quote figures — thirty per cent less audit preparation,
half the onboarding time — derived from someone else's operation under conditions you cannot
inspect. Those numbers are not measurements; they are marketing with a decimal point.

nance.it tracks planned versus real hours natively. Your return is measured **on your own
operation**, within the first year after implementation, using data the system produces as a
by-product of running. What follows is where that return comes from, and which number in the
system tells you whether it materialised.

## Efficiency gains

**Onboarding — time-to-competence goes down.** Explicit procedures plus a built-in training
path replace informal shadowing. A new engineer has something to learn *from* on day one.

*Measured by:* the interval between a person being registered and their first certified
[Onboarding](../talent/onboarding.md) record on a competence — compared against your current
ramp-up time.

**Capacity — planned versus real utilisation becomes visible.** Utilisation stops being an
estimate reconstructed from timesheets.

*Measured by:* [Capacity](../control/capacity.md) against
[Performance](../control/performance.md) — what the organisation can execute versus what it
did, per department.

**Audit preparation — hours per cycle go down.** Evidence is already linked to every
execution, so preparation becomes retrieval rather than reconstruction.

*Measured by:* the hours your team currently books against audit preparation, compared after
the first cycle run inside the platform.

## Risk and quality

**SLA breaches and contract exposure go down.** Execution is gated by active contracts: a
ticket can only trigger events covered by an [SLA](../crm/sla.md) with that department.

*Measured by:* tickets blocked at creation for lack of contractual coverage — each one is a
breach that did not happen.

**Knowledge is retained when key people leave.** This is the hidden cost nobody budgets,
because it never appears as a line item — it appears as a project that suddenly takes longer.

*Measured by:* the share of your critical tasks that have a registered
[Procedure](../operation/procedures.md) and more than one certified executor.

**Rework and nonconformities go down.** Only certified people, and the correct procedure for
the requirement set that applies.

*Measured by:* jobs whose real execution time exceeds planned, and — once the Quality module
ships — nonconformity closure time.

## Avoided cost

**Zero licence fees.** Apache 2.0, with no per-seat scaling penalty. The cost of adding the
hundredth user is the same as the tenth.

**No duplicated tooling.** Quality, governance and knowledge live in one platform rather than
three that must be kept in sync by hand.

**Features funded once, used by every unit.** Through the [shared roadmap](roadmap.md), the
capabilities other adopters pay for arrive in your instance at no cost.

## How to establish your baseline

The measurements above only mean something against a starting point, and the honest moment to
capture one is *before* implementation begins — during the
[discovery workshop](implementation.md#step-1-discovery-workshop), while your key users are
already in the room.

Four numbers are usually enough: current time-to-competence for a new engineer, hours booked
on the last audit cycle, the share of critical tasks with a written procedure, and current
rework volume. None requires new instrumentation. All four are things your managers can
estimate in an hour, and all four are things the platform will measure precisely afterwards.
